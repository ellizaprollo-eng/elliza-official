Speed to lead: the GoHighLevel follow-up I set up first

By Elliza Olvina · 2 min read · November 2026

Someone fills in your form. Right now they are thinking about your offer. An hour later they are thinking about something else. That is why the first workflow I build in a new GoHighLevel account is the speed-to-lead follow-up.

The trigger

The workflow starts when a lead comes in: a form or survey is submitted, a Facebook lead form arrives, or someone books from the website. One workflow per lead source keeps it easy to read and easy to fix.

Step 1: Save and sort the lead

The contact is created or updated, tagged with where they came from, and added to the pipeline in a "New lead" stage. From this moment nobody has to wonder where a lead is.

Step 2: Reply within the first minute

The lead gets a short text and an email right away. It comes from a real name, sounds like a person, and gives one next step, usually a booking link. Something like this:

Hi Sam, this is Elliza. Thanks for reaching out! Here is my calendar if you want to pick a time to talk: [link]

Texts only go to people who agreed to receive them, and US numbers need A2P 10DLC registration before SMS will deliver properly.

Step 3: Tell the business

An internal notification goes to the owner or the sales person, with the lead's name, phone and source. I also create a task to call them, so the lead does not depend on someone remembering.

Step 4: Follow up until they answer

If there is no reply, a few short messages go out over the next days. They are spaced out, they each give a reason to answer, and they stop as soon as the lead replies or books. That stop is the most important setting in the whole workflow. Nobody likes getting a reminder after they already said yes.

Step 5: When they book

The opportunity moves to "Booked", the lead gets a confirmation, and reminders go out before the call. If they do not show up, a separate workflow offers a new time.

Mistakes I avoid

  • No stop on reply, so leads get messages after they have answered.
  • Texts going out in the middle of the night. I set a send-time window.
  • Messages that sound like a robot. Short and human wins.
  • Skipping the test. I always run a test contact through every path before going live.

It is a simple workflow, and that is the point. Get this one right first, then build the fancy things on top of it.
