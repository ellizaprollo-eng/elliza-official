The five parts every lead funnel needs before you spend on ads

By Elliza Olvina · 2 min read · November 2026

Ads bring people to a page. The page decides if they become leads. Before I turn on any traffic, I go through the same five checks. If one of them is missing, the ad budget leaks.

1. One clear promise at the top

The first screen should say who the page is for and what they get. One headline, one short line under it, and one button. If a visitor has to scroll to understand the offer, most of them will not scroll.

2. Proof close to the ask

People trust other people more than they trust a headline. I place proof right next to the form or the button: reviews, client logos, before and after photos, or a short video. Only real proof. A made-up number does more damage than no number at all.

3. A short form

Every extra field costs leads. I ask only for what the business needs to follow up, usually a name and an email or phone number. If the business needs to qualify people, I add a second step after the first submit, so the lead is already saved.

4. Follow-up that starts right away

A funnel does not end at the form. The thank-you page should tell people what happens next, and the first message should reach them within minutes. In GoHighLevel I connect the form to a workflow that sends a text and an email, notifies the business and moves the lead into the pipeline. I wrote about that setup in Speed to lead.

5. Tracking you can trust

If you cannot see which ad brought which lead, you cannot fix anything. I set up the Meta pixel or Google tags to fire on the form submit and the booking, add UTM tags to every ad link, and then test it all with a real submission before launch.

My quick pre-launch checklist

  • Open the page on a phone. Most visitors will.
  • Check the load speed. A slow page loses people before they read a word.
  • Submit the form yourself and make sure the follow-up arrives.
  • Click every button and link.
  • Add the privacy and consent text the form needs.

None of this is fancy. It is the boring part that makes the exciting part, the ads, actually pay.
